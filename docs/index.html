Carbon Monoxide Oxygen Analyser

A low-cost DIY analyser that measures oxygen and carbon monoxide in diving gas at the same time, built around an ESP32-C3.

View the code on GitHub Read the original write-up
Warning: this is a cheap, poorly tested DIY build with rough code, not a commercial product. The CO sensor's maker states it must not be used for life support. Think about what your life is worth before you rely on one.

Why

CO analysers sold for diving cost far more than comparable industrial safety analysers. So I built a dual analyser that tests a gas for O2 and CO in one go. The O2 side reuses a standard R22D oxygen cell that came out of my CCR after 12 months, which still has plenty of life left.

What it does

O2 and CO together

One sample, both readings: O2 % and CO ppm in large type, with the O2 cell's millivolts and the CO sensor's temperature.

Steady O2 reading

The O2 cell is read through a 16-bit ADC and averaged over 40 samples.

One-button calibration

Calibrate on air or pure oxygen; the analyser works out which from the cell's output and remembers it.

Factory-calibrated CO

Electrochemical CO sensor: 0–1000 ppm at 1 ppm resolution, expected life over 2 years.

Rechargeable

Runs on an 18650 cell, charged through a USB-C socket on the case.

Simple wiring

The display, ADC and CO sensor all share one I2C bus.

Calibration

Press CAL while the O2 cell is exposed to the calibration gas. The firmware decides which gas it is from the cell's output:

Cell outputTreated asCalibration value
7–13 mVAir20.7% O2
Above 44 mVOxygen98% O2
Anything elseErrorShows CAL ERROR and stops until restarted

The calibration is saved to flash and loaded at start-up. On the very first start, the analyser calibrates straight away.

Build and flash

The firmware is a basic Arduino-framework C++ program, written in VS Code with PlatformIO. It should port to other IDEs.

pio run -t upload
pio device monitor

Libraries: U8g2, Adafruit ADS1X15, RunningAverage and EasyButton, installed by PlatformIO. The DFRobot multi-gas sensor library (MIT licence) is included in src.

3D-print files (STL and Fusion 360): 3D-Printed-Assembly.zip

Known limitations

  • The code could do with some optimisation.
  • It's lightly tested. Check it against a known gas before trusting a reading.
  • CO temperature compensation is turned off in the firmware.
